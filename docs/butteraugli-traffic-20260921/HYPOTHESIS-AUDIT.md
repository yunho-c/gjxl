# Final hypothesis audit

Scope: warm single-image, large e7/e10 resident encoding on this M4 Pro, preserving existing numerical and output contracts. This is an empirical investigation of the current Butteraugli implementation. It cannot enumerate all future algorithms or establish a mathematical hardware optimum. The primary checkout remains unchanged. The investigation is complete at the user-approved practical stopping point; this is not a proof of universal optimality.

| Mechanism | Evidence and disposition |
|---|---|
| Taller 33-tap tiles | Fourteen initial geometries; isolated wins did not consistently translate to ordinary encoding. Later tile/reuse interaction screen has 23 configurations. The supported two-output tile has independent complete-call evidence; four-output finalists have mixed ordinary increments. |
| Retain halos across stripes | Eight initial rolling variants, two-axis followups, and four final rolling interactions. Gains are shape/extent dependent; none beats the best single-stripe alternative across both screened large extents. No qualified incremental encoder improvement. |
| Adjacent horizontal outputs | Twelve conditional implementations regress. Eight branch-free refinements identify a useful exact-load-sharing mechanism, retained in the supported bundle. |
| Adjacent vertical outputs | Four two-axis and four larger-reuse variants plus the geometry interaction screen. Two vertical outputs are retained. Four-output alternatives improve isolated/stage timing but lack consistent ordinary incremental evidence; eight outputs are worse than four in corresponding shapes. |
| Weights/normalization/launch bounds | Twelve normalization variants add no demonstrated benefit over geometry. Explicit 256/384-thread bounds reproduce unbounded optimized controls within about 0.15 percentage points in the final micro-screen. |
| 7-tap ultra filtering | Eight direct-filter layouts identify substantial isolated gains; disjoint-input integration and its epilogue pass broader contracts/byte/decoder checks and reduce ultra stage time 31-35%. Retained in the supported bundle. |
| 13/15-tap direct filtering | Sixteen initial layouts regress. The ten-configuration short-filter followup passes guarded parity and reverses the earlier outcome: approximately 50-54% less isolated 13/15-tap time. Independent high/mask/medium-B integrations pass seven focused tests and exact paired outputs. High reduces ordinary encoding 2.7-2.9% in three-pair screens; medium-B and mask ordinary results are mixed at 48 MP. The combined candidate passes 56 byte comparisons and three identical finite decoded pairs; seven-pair original-baseline confirmation improves ordinary time 12.697%/10.715%, and all 18 broad large-image pairs improve. The independent high-only ablation confirms the joint medium-B/mask addition. The 21-configuration interaction and 18-configuration horizontal-reuse screens lead to a final refinement with completed correctness, confirmation, controls and broad coverage. Its incremental ordinary effect is small and workload dependent; closed at the practical stopping point. |
| 5-tap Opsin geometry | The current tiled blur-plus-Opsin implementation already removes horizontal full-image intermediates. The historical 16x8 choice balanced smaller inputs; the historical 32x8 lead has mixed ordinary increments (-0.77% at 24 MP, +0.07% at 48 MP). No consistent broad gain established. |
| Remaining 13-tap transpose geometry | The 16x16 shape from the earlier selective-plane study has targeted stage gains but negligible ordinary increments (-0.025%/-0.265%). No qualified broad gain. This is distinct from the new direct shared-load tile. |
| DC producer composition | Main and subscale prototypes pass guarded producer/reducer checks. Main-only ordinary gain is small; combined AC/DC/filter/ultra bundle has robust seven-pair complete-call improvement. Retained. |
| AC composition in Malta | Reduction/sub-final become faster while Malta becomes approximately 8% slower. All moved work is included in the 27.6-29.5% total Butteraugli improvement of the final original-baseline comparison. Retained. |
| Pixel materialization and regular composition | Separate materialization raises reduction scope 26-42%. Fusing composition into the existing mask producer still raises mask-plus-reduction 6-9% in both grid orientations. Guarded exact output passes; reject both designs for these workloads. |
| Reduction coordinates and tree | Current production 8x8 SIMD tail preserves the original tree. Exact power-of-two addressing is flat. Prior larger-tree studies were mixed; arbitrary reassociation would change arithmetic and require separate qualification. |
| Reference mask invariants | Raw reference masks are already cached in current source. Optimistic nonlinear-curve consumer caches save less than 0.9% of their scopes before preparation/storage cost; the fixed-rest whole-call estimate is around 0.1%, not a theoretical bound. No new cache justified by these measurements. |
| Malta neighborhood geometry | Twelve geometries and eight adjacent-output refinements preserve guarded values. Larger tiles save approximately 3.5% in integrated Malta, but ordinary incremental results are mixed. No qualified additional complete-call gain over the supported bundle. |
| Precision/representation changes | Live prior precision report records failed blur-scratch quality gates, weak half-mask benefits, and retained targeted Malta reciprocal arithmetic already in baseline. No tolerances were widened here. Merely aliasing dead arena planes changes capacity, not necessarily warm traffic. |
| Launch-only/global fusion | Live prior fusion study tested six-way Malta and channel fusions without a dependable benefit. This study's successful changes remove repeated requests or simplify dataflow, not merely dispatch count. General cross-kernel algorithms remain possible research directions; they are not ruled out by these finite experiments. |

The main `short-bundle` meets all focused correctness, pinned decoder, broad 12/24/48 MP ordinary checks, seven-pair flagship confirmation, ablation and same-binary control requirements. It confirms 12.697%/10.715% less ordinary latency and 29.513%/27.598% less Butteraugli GPU time at 24/48 MP. The independent seven-pair high-only ablation confirms 2.104%/2.299% ordinary gains from the joint medium-B/mask addition.

Campaigns 17–20 resolve the remaining concrete short-filter leads. The final combined refinement independently passes seven focused tests, 56 byte comparisons and three identical finite decoded pairs. Against `short-bundle`, confirmation gives -1.220% at 24 MP (7/7) and -0.296% at 48 MP (5/7); all six broad large-image medians improve 0.24–1.35%, with many mixed individual pairs. Matching unchanged-binary controls are +0.284%/+0.209%, and small-image results are mixed. These controls diagnose variability; they are not corrections. No pending measurement remains.

**Final disposition:** practical local opportunities have been investigated sufficiently to stop under the user's explicit practical-stopping preference. The substantial gains are established; the last retained prototype has smaller, less general incremental evidence. Sub-percent and workload-specific improvements remain possible, as do new algorithms. Those possibilities do not justify a claim of zero headroom or require indefinite tuning under the agreed stopping rule. An independent audit verified 23 late-study cohorts, 256 pairs and 3,356 file hashes; the restored final prototype re-passed seven tests and matches its frozen shader.
