These counts are logical accesses, not measured DRAM traffic or a proof of a bandwidth ceiling. Cache reuse and writeback domains matter.

For the main-scale low/medium filter, the production 16x64 tile computes 96 horizontal rows. Interior pixels therefore incur 1.5 horizontal outputs per final output. Three channels, 33 taps and four-byte samples imply 594 bytes of horizontal source requests per output pixel, plus 12 bytes for center inputs and 24 bytes for six output stores. Unique image content is only 12 input plus 24 output bytes per pixel. The very large difference means source-level load counts must not be equated to external bandwidth. Halo reuse can reduce both repeated arithmetic and L1/load-instruction demand while barely changing unique DRAM bytes.

The current resident L2 reducer reads 21 full-resolution FP32 planes: eight reference features, eight distorted features, two Malta accumulators, the reference eroded mask, and two blurred masks. It also reads one quarter-area subscale map. The unique payload is approximately 85 bytes per source pixel before anchor/block/score traffic, if each plane is read once and the subscale read is reused across its four pixels. At 24 MP that is about 2.04 GB per evaluation. This is a useful data-volume model, not a measured compulsory DRAM bound: earlier producers and subsequent consumers may reuse cache lines.

The source audit identified a cross-stage candidate beyond launch/reduction-tree tuning: distorted low-frequency outputs are consumed by the final L2 term. In a resident-only path, low/medium production reads the three prepared reference low planes and reference mask, computes the exact masked DC sum, and stores one combined value instead of three distorted low planes. The reducer then consumes that value instead of six reference/distorted low planes and omits its DC-mask arithmetic. The naive combined accounting saves roughly 12 bytes per pixel across producer and consumer after charging the earlier reference/mask reads. The main-scale prototype is implemented in `dc-fusion-source/` and frozen in `integrated/dc-fusion/`. Three focused tests under Metal validation and paired 24/48 MP codestream identity pass. A three-pair stage screen shows approximately 24% less resident reduction time and 8-9% less combined filtering/reduction time. Complete-call confirmation, broader correctness, subscale extension, and combinations remain open; this is not a qualified production change.

Other unclosed candidates include regular-pixel materialization followed by block reduction (extra map traffic traded for coalescing), reference-only mask-curve preparation, remaining 7/13/15-tap filtering, and Malta neighborhood reuse. Existing fusion/half-storage results must be reviewed before repeating those mechanisms.

## September 22 refinement accounting

The AC/DC representation reduces the main reducer's full-resolution inputs from 21 to eight FP32 planes: packed DC; reference/distorted medium-B; two AC accumulators; eroded mask; and reference/distorted blurred masks. Including the quarter-area map gives approximately 33 logical bytes per original pixel, versus 85 in the original reducer. This is a **consumer-only** accounting. AC fusion moves high/medium reads into the final Malta passes; those moved reads must be charged there. DC fusion's approximately 12-byte/pixel net source-level saving remains the defensible combined producer/consumer accounting, rather than claiming the entire 52-byte reducer decrease as eliminated memory traffic.

Two-axis load sharing changes repeated request counts more than unique image data. At 16x64, two adjacent horizontal outputs share 34 source values per channel rather than issuing 66 loads. Charging the 96 horizontal rows gives 306 logical source-request bytes per final pixel versus 594. At 16x96 the halo factor falls from 1.5 to 4/3, giving 272 bytes. Four adjacent vertical outputs share 36 values per channel rather than issuing 132 threadgroup loads, or 108 versus 396 logical threadgroup-request bytes per final pixel. Each output retains its original ascending tap accumulation and final normalization. These are ideal interior source counts, not compiler instruction counts, cache misses, or measured DRAM bandwidth.

The 16x64/four-output design uses 256 threads and 18,432 bytes of threadgroup storage; 16x96/four-output uses 384 threads and 24,576 bytes. The latter's approximately 39% isolated improvement does not imply a 39% encoder gain. Larger live accumulator sets and storage footprints trade against concurrency, and eight-output variants are slower than the four-output versions in the measured screen.

The new mask-producer composition experiment writes main distance where the completed distorted mask previously lived. The next reducer then reads that value plus the subscale map. Unlike separate materialization, it adds no dispatch and replaces a store instead of adding a map store. Most feature reads are moved into the mask pass; the direct logical saving is the eliminated later distorted-mask read (four bytes/pixel), not the whole decrease in reducer input bytes. The two grid orientations test the competing locality demands of transposed blur input and row-major feature inputs. All mask work and reduction work must be added before interpreting a benefit. These candidates remain unqualified until their guarded and integrated measurements complete.

## Short-filter shared-load accounting

For an interior one-channel blur of K taps, the old two-pass path requests K input samples and K full-image intermediate samples per output, plus intermediate/output stores. The new 16x64 tile shares two adjacent horizontal outputs, computing (64+K-1) rows; its ideal horizontal input-request bytes per final pixel are `4 * (K+1)/2 * (64+K-1)/64`: 17.5 bytes for 7 taps, 33.25 for 13, and 39 for 15. These replace 28/52/60 logical horizontal source-request bytes. The vertical pass reads threadgroup memory, sharing K+3 values across four outputs, and avoids the full-image horizontal intermediate. These counts exclude center reads and nonlinear epilogue outputs in production high/ultra kernels. They are source requests, not compiler instruction counts or measured external-memory traffic.

Packing medium B alone into its difference value would move one reference read into the producer and replace the two consumer feature reads with one packed read. After charging that moved producer read, it does not itself reduce the number of full-image map accesses. Such a representation change is not justified merely by a smaller consumer timer; the successful short-filter integration instead removes the transpose intermediate without moving nonlinear work out of the measured scope.
